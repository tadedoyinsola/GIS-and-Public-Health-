Childhood Lead Exposure and Housing in Allegheny County, Pennsylvania

A tract-level spatial epidemiological analysis of elevated childhood blood lead cases across Allegheny County's 416 census tracts, examining three housing indicators (housing era, market quality, and condition) against the geography of childhood lead exposure burden. Includes formal spatial autocorrelation analysis (Global Moran's I) and Local Indicators of Spatial Association (LISA) cluster identification.

The headline result: Lead exposure clusters in central Allegheny County tracts whose median housing structure was built before 1942. Housing complaints provide the strongest indicator of which tracts within those high-risk neighborhoods are experiencing active exposure — and complaints capture information not fully explained by housing era or rent alone. Federal lead-remediation targeting should integrate both housing-age and complaint data for highest geographic precision.

Methodology

How the analysis was constructed

The analysis joins five data sources to 416 Census tract polygons, computes quantile-classified choropleths for three housing indicators, and applies formal spatial autocorrelation testing (Global Moran's I) followed by Local Indicators of Spatial Association (LISA) cluster decomposition.

Data Sources

Lead cases
Allegheny County Health Department surveillance data, 2000 vintage — count of children with elevated blood lead levels aggregated to census tract.
Housing complaints
ACHD complaint registry — count of housing-related complaints aggregated to census tract.
Housing era
U.S. Census 2000 SF3 — median year structure built, by census tract. Direct proxy for pre-1978 lead-paint era housing stock.
Median gross rent
U.S. Census 2000 SF3 Table H063 — median gross rent in 2000 USD, by census tract.
Tract boundaries
U.S. Census TIGER/Line 2010, year-2000 vintage tract polygons for Allegheny County (FIPS 42003).

Statistical Methods

Pearson product-moment correlations quantify pairwise relationships between lead cases and each housing indicator. Comparison-group statistics test the difference between tracts with 50+ lead cases vs. tracts with zero cases, following the textbook (Kurland & Gorr) methodology.

Spatial autocorrelation testing uses Global Moran's I with row-standardized Queen contiguity spatial weights and 999-permutation pseudo-significance testing. LISA cluster decomposition identifies tract-level statistically significant cluster types (High-High hotspots, Low-Low cold spots) and spatial outliers (Low-High, High-Low).

Cartographic Conventions

All choropleths use quantile classification (5 classes). Sequential color schemes for continuous indicators (purple gradient); diverging colors for the LISA classification (red hotspot, blue cold spot). The graduated-point overlay encodes lead case count via circle radius, with consistent yellow fill for visual continuity across all indicator views.

National importance and policy applications

EPA's national strategy designates geographic targeting of lead-exposure interventions toward older, lower-quality housing stock as a central federal priority. Direct Policy Applications

The 48 LISA High-High hotspot tracts identified in this analysis represent the geographic concentration where federal lead-remediation funding would yield the highest population-health return on investment. Additionally, the finding that housing complaints provide non-redundant information beyond housing era suggests that integrating local health department complaint registries with federal census-based housing data improves targeting precision — a methodological insight directly applicable to other U.S. metropolitan areas with similar industrial-era housing stock.

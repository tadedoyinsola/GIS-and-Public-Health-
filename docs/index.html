Spatial Epidemiology Interactive

Kentucky Lung Cancer Mortality, 2000–2004

A county-level analysis of lung cancer mortality across Kentucky, exploring race-stratified mortality rates and identifying cross-population priority counties.

Key finding: Knox and Gallatin counties identified as the only Kentucky counties exceeding the 150-per-100,000 mortality threshold for both Black and White males simultaneously.

Health Insurance Geography Static

California Uninsured and Minority Populations

A three-panel comparative analysis of uninsured rates and minority population distributions across California's 58 counties, with statistical correlation analysis.

Headline result: Uninsured rate is strongly correlated with Hispanic population share (r = 0.72) but completely independent of Black population share (r = 0.00) — a finding with direct implications for coverage-expansion outreach strategy.

Environmental Health Dashboard

Allegheny County Childhood Lead Exposure

A spatial epidemiological analysis of elevated childhood blood lead cases against three housing indicators (era, market quality, condition), with formal Moran's I spatial autocorrelation testing and LISA cluster identification.

Methodological centerpiece: Global Moran's I = 0.465 (p < 0.001) formally validates the lead-exposure clustering; LISA decomposition identifies 48 statistically significant hotspot tracts. Directly supports EPA Lead Strategy and Federal Lead Action Plan targeting priorities.

Injury Surveillance & Prevention Dashboard

Pittsburgh Child Pedestrian Injury Geography

A neighborhood-level spatial analysis pairing childhood injury surveillance with proximity to playgrounds, schools, and convenience stores. Three sub-analyses synthesized into one decision framework for prevention programming.

Methodological centerpiece: Demonstrates buffer-based proximity analysis at 600 ft and 1,200 ft, identifying that the school-zone injury rate (33.11 per 10,000 children) is 62% higher than the broader school neighborhood — supporting U.S. DOT Safe Streets for All targeting priorities.

About

Tolulope Adedoyin Oladeji is a PhD student in Geography and GIS at the University of Cincinnati and a Graduate Research Assistant at the Digital Epidemiology Laboratory. Her research integrates geospatial AI, remote sensing, and spatial epidemiology, with applied work in maternal and child health vulnerability mapping across low- and middle-income countries.

The projects on this page are part of a broader portfolio demonstrating reproducible geospatial public health workflows in open-source Python tooling — each one re-implementing a substantive analysis end-to-end, with full code, methodology, and interpretive discussion.

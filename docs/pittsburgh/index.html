Four Key Findings

01

Schools have the steepest distance-decay

The injury rate within 600 ft of schools (33.11 per 10,000 children) is 62% higher than the rate within 1,200 ft (20.41). Convenience stores show a much shallower decay (28.65 vs 25.41) — risk is more diffuse around commercial corridors.

02

Garfield and Upper Lawrenceville are the defensible priorities

Garfield combines the highest absolute injury count (9) with a moderate rate (4.48). Upper Lawrenceville has both meaningful count (5) and high rate (7.33). Together they represent the cleanest evidence-based targets for prevention programming.

03

The highest rates are statistical noise

Hays, Bluff, and Mt. Oliver show "very high" rates above 9 per 1,000, but each is driven by a single injury in a small child population. These extreme rates reflect denominator instability, not real priority targets. The visual map can be misleading without the underlying counts.

04

Schools and stores capture different injury geographies

The 1,200 ft school buffer contains 49 injuries; the equivalent store buffer contains 50. But the underlying youth populations differ (24,005 vs 19,677). A single-destination prevention strategy misses meaningful injury exposure happening near the other destination type.

Data & Methodology

Three sub-assignments synthesized into one analysis: proximity buffers around playgrounds (Assignment 7-1), buffers around schools and convenience stores (Assignment 7-2), and a neighborhood-level injury rate choropleth (Assignment 7-3). All buffers were computed in NAD83 Pennsylvania South State Plane to ensure distances are accurate in feet.
